Case study 01

A queue you can actually read

Support engineers were triaging 400 tickets a day in a table with 19 columns. I designed and shipped the replacement.

Role
Design + backend
Team
Me, 1 frontend
Span
11 weeks
Result
−38% handle time
[ hero image placeholder ]
final triage view — 1600×900
01 — Research
I sat with six agents for a full shift each.

Every one of them had built the same private workaround: sort by age, ignore twelve columns, keep a paper list of the accounts that matter. The table wasn't slow. It was undecidable.

02 — Framing

Reframed the job from "show all ticket data" to "answer one question: what do I open next?" Everything else became progressive disclosure.

"I don't need more information. I need permission to ignore most of it." — agent, week 2
03 — Explorations

Three directions, tested as clickable prototypes over two weeks.

[ A — kanban ]
Died on volume.
[ B — one at a time ]
Fast, but no overview.
[ C — shipped ]
Ranked list + peek.
04 — Build

The ranking needed a service that didn't exist, so I wrote it: a scoring endpoint in Go that folded SLA risk, account tier and reopen count into one number. Designing it and owning the query plan turned out to be the same job.

05 — Results
−38%
median handle time
19→4
columns on screen
0
paper lists left
06 — What I'd change

I shipped the ranking as a black box. Agents trusted it for a month, then stopped when it got one call wrong. Next time the score explains itself on the row.

Case study 01

Ledger for freelancers

Role
Lead designer
Team
2 eng, 1 PM
Year
2026
Outcome
+22% activation

Sole traders were abandoning invoices halfway through. Not because the form was long, but because it asked for things they did not have yet.

[ image placeholder ]
before / after of the invoice flow — 1600×900
Fig. 1 — the old flow, annotated during a research session.
The problem
We watched nine freelancers invoice a real client. Seven of them stopped at the tax field and opened a browser tab. The form was not too long — it was in the wrong order.
What we did
Moved everything the user could not answer from memory behind a single deferred step, and let a draft invoice exist without it.
